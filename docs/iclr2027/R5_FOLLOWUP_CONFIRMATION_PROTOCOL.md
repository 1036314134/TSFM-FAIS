# Frozen follow-up after matched portfolio development

Registered on 14 September 2026 before any follow-up forecasting. The original 373-window natural confirmation was examined and did not reproduce the old method's development advantage. It remains a used evaluation set. The follow-up concerns a changed, explicitly versioned method; it does not replace or relabel that result.

The primary method is the pairwise 35-triple selector trained against the complete-history teacher error of each actual forecast median (`median_risk`). Its matched control uses mean individual-member teacher error (`member_risk`). Both use the same 40 visible features and 595 cost-sensitive pair classifiers, the same seven candidate forecasts, and the existing learning parameters. Four final models are frozen in `followup-source-bundle-v001` using only the 165 source training histories from 15 families. No source validation or follow-up observations are used to fit these models. The 43-option regression prototype remains a failed development experiment.

Chronos-2 makes one joint choice for the two target variables; TimesFM 2.5 chooses independently for each target. Input length and horizon are both 96 sampling steps. Candidate input values remain on their original scale; the primary MAE and MSE use observed training-prefix means and population standard deviations. Original future missing values never become ground truth. The numerical targets remain the first two columns in the source ordering. Results on other target choices are outside this registration.

Candidate filling uses the unchanged six algorithms, historical fallback rules, neural architectures, and 10-epoch/64-window prefix training recipe. The seventh action is the supported native-missing forecast with the existing empty-input fallback. Fitting is restricted to the first 20% of each selected item. All evaluations and any masked contexts occur after the 60% time boundary. The resource queue remains third priority with automatic yielding; it sends a Windows notification on exit.

The primary comparison is against the seven-candidate forecast median. The matched member-risk selector, frozen source-fixed triple, old individual-teacher top-three rule, seven individual actions, finite-six median, and forecast means are controls. Additional output or input controls must be declared before forecasting and cannot be selected after their accuracy is observed. Seven distinct candidate contexts are the maximum primary query budget, with identical effective inputs eligible for deduplication. Learned selection costs are additional to forecasting and must not be hidden.

Three panels are kept separate. First, the unused items in Water Quality Darwin, SG Weather, and Smart Manufacturing identified by `fresh-confirmation-inventory-v001` contribute all eligible origins. These are held-out items in previously examined families, not new families; the inventory currently has 202 windows from 35 items, including 95 missing contexts in two families. Second, UCI Air Quality and UCI Individual Household Electric Power Consumption are candidate new-source natural panels. Their numerical columns and original order are retained, including Air Quality's -200 sentinel and Household Power's empty or question-mark entries as missing. Within each item, retain at most 16 eligible missing contexts and 16 eligible complete contexts, selecting evenly spaced indices separately in each chronological stratum. This is a conditional, mask-stratified panel, not an estimate of the population missingness rate. Ineligible sources are reported with reasons and are not replaced based on predictive performance. Third, the local weather release and the pinned Alabama solar release form a separate synthetic panel. Choose eight evenly spaced eligible origins per item with fully observed input and two-target future; use all six existing missingness mechanisms, rates 0.1/0.3/0.5, and new seeds 7101/7102. Generate a whole-trajectory mask for each configuration with prefix-only calibration, then take the registered windows. This yields at most 576 synthetic cases. Mechanism seed derivation and realized context masks are saved before forecasting.

The common origin grid is the existing `confirmation_windows` rule: stride 192; each prefix channel has at least two original observations; each target future has at least 48 original observations; the first origin is the maximum of the 60% boundary plus 96 and the prefix end plus 288. Solar has no supplied calendar timestamps and uses relative sequence positions with a 10-minute sampling interval. No calendar start is invented. UCI timestamps must be present, ordered and unique; absent regular-grid times, if any, are explicitly inserted as unobserved. Empty CSV padding is removed; measurement rows with missing values are preserved.

Before input preparation, freeze the exact cohort and raw-file hashes, check the new source IDs against the development catalog and old confirmation, exclude used item IDs, and check exact target-history/future duplicates against the previous source and confirmation windows. Preserve exclusions. Equality of data sources to pretraining corpora cannot generally be established from these checks: new to this experiment does not mean absent from a forecasting model's pretraining. The MoTM reference explicitly includes Solar among its training sources.

Report paired MAE and MSE differences by item and family, with macro-averaging across windows, items and families. The synthetic panel additionally retains mechanism/rate/seed breakdowns; its 36 masks per history do not count as 36 independent histories. Natural missing-context results are primary within natural panels, with complete-context controls separate. Confidence intervals resample at the family level where enough families exist; two-family panels require explicit uncertainty limits and their individual-family outcomes. No claim of broad or consistent gain follows from a favorable pooled number alone. Inspecting these results makes this cohort used; subsequent model changes require a separately declared evaluation role.
