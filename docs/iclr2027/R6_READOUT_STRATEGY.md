# R6 result interpretation fixed before accuracy readout

Retain the primary horizon 96 and secondary horizon 192 separately. The principal artificial-missingness panel contains four source families. The original-NA panel contains one Beijing source family with multiple stations; nominal time-grid gaps and complete-release controls remain separate. All 23 methods are reported, with the fixed ensemble-loss gate compared against each of the other 22 outputs. No method, source, horizon or missingness subset is selected after observing its accuracy.

The reporting hierarchy is target, masks of the same origin, origins within an item, items within a dataset, and datasets within a source family. Registered equal mask multiplicity must make the origin-first and window-first means agree. Aggregate relative change is the ratio of the two family-macro errors minus one; it is not the mean of source-specific percentage changes. Original source-specific values, origin-level errors, and mechanism/rate/seed breakdowns remain available.

For at least three observed source families, retain central 95% ranges from 20,000 paired source-family resamplings with seed 9101. These ranges describe sensitivity to reweighting the observed sources; the four purposively selected families do not support a broad population confidence claim. Fewer than three families produce no range. Do not label an interval excluding zero as proof of significance, and do not report unadjusted significance discoveries among the multiple comparisons. Report source-level joint MAE/MSE wins and losses beside the aggregate effects.

The validation readout checks 11 structural, inferential and causal interpretation risks. In particular, original future observability conditions the evaluated population; missing and complete context strata do not estimate real-world missingness prevalence; station, horizon and mask counts are not independent family counts; and differences between forecasters do not isolate one architectural cause. All original failures and weaker conditions are retained. Computational algorithm comparisons establish effects for the fixed evaluation cases, with extrapolation requiring additional evidence.
