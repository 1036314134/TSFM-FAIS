\lhead{Peer Repair and Forecast Fusion}

\begin{abstract}
Forecasting after a sensor outage requires deciding how missing recent values should interact with the longer observed history. We present a fixed procedure that repairs recent target gaps using regressions fitted on observed local and related-series prefixes, retains the available long context of a frozen Chronos-2 forecaster, and equally combines its point prediction with a prefix-fitted vector autoregression. The procedure preserves observed and auxiliary entries and requires one foundation-model forecast per origin. On 45 natural-outage windows from 12 Beijing air-quality stations, it attains standardized MAE/MSE of 0.388053/0.244688. Relative to native prediction using the same long history and VAR fusion, the reductions are 5.98\%/7.26\%; relative to long native prediction alone, they are 19.96\%/31.90\%. Gaussian repair with the same fusion performs nearly identically, while other controls are stronger under artificial outages and at horizon 96. Available Gaussian and fusion components also fail to improve natural-gap forecasting on an HDB parking cohort. These developmental results identify an effective fixed procedure for the studied natural sensor outages and delimit its current evidence across missingness conditions and applications.
\end{abstract}
